The small light

The keeper climbed the stair with the oil can in one hand and the other on the rail, counting as she always did, though she had known the number since she was nine.

At the top the lamp room was cold and smelled of brass polish. She set the can down, wiped the lens with her sleeve, and looked out at water that gave nothing back. Somewhere past the bar a boat was late.

She struck the match on the third try. The flame was small, and for a moment it seemed a foolish thing to offer the dark.

Then the lens caught it and threw it out over the harbour, and the light was no longer hers.
